Commitment

Ten percent, where the contract is signed.

On every payment we receive, 10% goes to an association in the territory where the contract was signed. The client chooses which one. Amounts are published. This page sets out the exact mechanism, what it genuinely costs us, and what it is not.

How it works, precisely

1
You choose the association when signing, from public-interest organisations in your territory. If you have no preference, we suggest a list and you decide.
2
You pay the agreed price. The donation is not added to your invoice — it comes out of our share.
3
We give 10% of the amount received, within the month following payment — grouped into a single quarterly transfer when the bank fees would otherwise eat into the association's share.
4
The payment is published on this page: association, amount, date. Verifiable.
One detail that matters: 10% of what we receive, not of what we invoice. An unpaid invoice triggers no donation, and money we merely pass through — ad budgets, third-party licences, hardware bought on your behalf — is deducted before the calculation, because it was never ours. That is what makes this sustainable rather than declarative: we do not promise a share of money we have not earned.

What it actually costs us

Corporate giving in New Caledonia earns a tax credit on the donation — 80% for payments made between 1 January 2024 and 31 December 2026, 60% before and after that window — but capped at 0.5% of turnover (tax code, article Lp 37-2 for companies, Lp 136-3 for individuals). We give 10%, which is twenty times that ceiling. The table below uses the 80% rate in force today.

The tax credit covers 4% of what is given — and 3% once the rate returns to 60% in 2027. The rest comes out of our margin, with no fiscal offset. Two conditions apply on top: being taxed under the régime réel, and the association having filed its statutes with the tax service.

Outside New Caledonia there is no offset at all. A donation to an association in Port Vila, Suva or Honiara opens no credit in any jurisdiction we operate from — in New Zealand the 33.33% donation credit only covers organisations approved by Inland Revenue. So on a regional contract, the ten percent are paid in full.

We publish this because it is the only way to tell a commitment from an optimisation. A scheme designed around the tax ceiling would sit at 0.5% — not at 10%.

Where the money goes

To the territory where the contract is signed. A New Caledonian client funds a New Caledonian association; a Port Vila client funds a Vanuatu one. Value stays where it is produced — which is also why we do not pool donations into a single organisation.

The areas we lean toward, without excluding others: environment and marine protection, education and youth, language and cultural heritage, employment pathways. If your association fits none of these but matters to you, it works for us.

A practical condition: for the payment to be traceable and receipted, the organisation must be of public interest and — in New Caledonia — have filed its statutes with the relevant service. We check this with you before contracting, which avoids unpleasant surprises on both sides.

What this is not

  • Not a surcharge on your invoice. The advertised price is the price paid. The 10% comes from our share, not yours.
  • Not a time-limited campaign. No end date, no counter, no manufactured urgency.
  • Not a tax arrangement. The figures above show it: the tax scheme covers 4% of the donation, and nothing outside New Caledonia. If the point were optimisation, we would give twenty times less.
  • Not a claim to virtue. We are not declaring ourselves exemplary. We take a percentage and we pay it out; it is a number, it is checkable, and that is all we say about it.

Common questions

Does the donation get added to my price?

No. The advertised price is what you pay. The 10% is taken from our share after payment is received. Your invoice is the same whether or not you choose an association.

Can I choose the association?

Yes — that is the point. You choose when signing. The only condition is that the organisation be of public interest and based in the territory where the contract is signed. If you have no preference, we suggest a list and you decide.

How do I know the payment was made?

Every payment is published on this page — association, amount, date — and the donation receipt is passed to you. A commitment announced without proof is worth nothing, which is why we would rather start with an empty page than an unverifiable number.

Does this actually cost you anything?

Yes. Corporate giving in New Caledonia earns a tax credit — 80% of the donation until the end of 2026, 60% after that — but capped at 0.5% of turnover, while we give 10%. The credit therefore covers 4% of what is given, and nothing at all on a contract signed outside New Caledonia. The rest comes out of our margin. That gap is precisely what separates a commitment from an arrangement.

What if I am in a territory you do not list?

The principle holds: 10% goes to an association in your territory, chosen by you. We simply check before contracting that the organisation can receive a donation from New Caledonia and issue a receipt.
