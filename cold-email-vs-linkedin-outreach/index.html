Channels

Cold email or LinkedIn? They answer different questions.

These are usually presented as rivals. They are better understood as tools with different failure modes — and the decision is less about which converts better than about which one you can still be using in a year.

The comparison

Cold emailLinkedIn
Who you can reachAnyone with a business addressOnly people active on the platform
Who owns the channelYou — your domain, your listThe platform. Access can be limited or removed
AutomationLegitimate, within volume and legal limitsAgainst platform terms; accounts get restricted
Cost at volumeDomain plus sending, near-flat as you growPremium tiers, priced per seat
Message lengthAs long as neededShort, and connection requests shorter still
Trade coverageStrong — most businesses have emailWeak in trades, hospitality, manufacturing

Where LinkedIn genuinely wins

  • Sectors that live on it. Software, consulting, recruitment, finance, marketing. If your buyer checks it daily, a well-judged message there gets seen faster than email.
  • Warm context. Shared connections, a group in common, a post they wrote. That context is visible and usable in a way it is not in email.
  • Research. Even if you never send a message there, it is the best source for understanding who does what inside a company.
  • Low commitment. A connection request is a smaller ask than an email, which suits long sales cycles where you are building familiarity over months.

Where cold email wins

  • Reach. An electrician in Whangārei, a boatbuilder in Northland, a winery on Waiheke — all have email; many have no meaningful platform presence.
  • You own it. Your domain and your list cannot be restricted by a policy change. Anyone who has had an account limited understands what that is worth.
  • It scales legitimately. Automating email within volume and legal limits is normal practice. Automating platform outreach violates the terms of service, and enforcement has become aggressive.
  • Room to be specific. A connection request gives you a sentence. An email gives you a paragraph — enough to show you understood their situation.

The honest recommendation

If your buyers are on the platform, use both: research and warm signal there, the substantive approach by email. If your buyers are trades, hospitality, marine, manufacturing or most local businesses, email is not the better channel — it is close to the only one.

The platform-risk asymmetry deserves weight. An email list you built is an asset you keep. A network on someone else's platform is access you are permitted, and permission can be withdrawn without appeal.

Common questions

Can I automate LinkedIn outreach?

Tools exist, and using them breaches the platform's terms of service. Enforcement has become considerably more aggressive, and restrictions typically hit the account you have spent years building. Email automation, kept within volume and legal limits, carries no equivalent risk of losing the channel itself.

Which gets better response rates?

It varies so much by industry and audience that a general answer would mislead. The more useful question is where your specific buyers actually are. For trades and local businesses, email reaches people the platform never will; for software and consulting, a warm platform approach often outperforms a cold email.

Should I message someone on both channels?

Sequentially and with a light touch — a connection request, then an email that does not pretend the first did not happen. Simultaneous messages on both channels reads as pressure and is a common reason people disengage entirely.

Is LinkedIn worth paying for if I mainly use email?

Often yes, for research rather than messaging. Identifying the right person to write to, and understanding what a company actually does, is where it earns its cost even if every message you send goes by email.
