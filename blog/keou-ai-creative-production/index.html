Keou: your visual production line, white-label and run at roughly 1% of the cost

Commercial product visuals still cost agencies real money and real hours — about $18 and 25 minutes each, so a 50-visual campaign is a 25-hour job. Keou turns a single product photo into commercial-grade visuals, videos and voiceovers in minutes, at AI-API cost, inside a platform that carries your brand — and hands your client a branded portal plus a savings report.

What is Keou?

Keou is a private, white-label AI creative-production platform for agencies. An agency uploads a product photo and generates commercial-grade visuals, videos, voiceovers and format variants in minutes, at AI-API cost — on the order of $0.09 per image instead of roughly $18 and 25 minutes the traditional way. Each deployment is a fully branded agency instance — its own logo, colours and domain — so the agency's clients never see Keou. It is live at keou.systems.

The problem: visual production is slow and expensive

Every agency that produces visuals for product, e-commerce or social clients runs into the same wall. A single commercial visual — a clean product shot, a lifestyle scene, a variant for a marketplace listing — costs on the order of $18 and about 25 minutes of skilled work between shooting, retouching and adapting. Scale that to a real brief and the maths gets ugly fast: a 50-visual product campaign is roughly 25 hours of work, before a single video or voiceover is even discussed.

That cost structure does two things to an agency. It caps how many briefs it can take on, and it squeezes margins on the briefs it does take. The bottleneck isn't talent — it's the slow, manual production line behind the talent.

What Keou does

Keou collapses that production line. An agency uploads a product photo and generates commercial-grade visuals, videos, voiceovers and format variants in minutes, at the cost of the underlying AI API — on the order of $0.09 per image rather than $18. It is white-label AI visual production: the platform carries the agency's logo, colours and domain, so to the agency's clients it simply looks like the agency's own studio.

This is AI product photography for agencies treated as infrastructure, not a toy. One deployment equals one fully branded agency instance, secure and self-contained, with the agency's team working inside it and the agency's clients reviewing the output through a branded portal.

The end-to-end workflow

Keou is a complete AI creative production platform, not a single generator. The workflow runs from team setup all the way to a client sign-off:

  • Team & quota management. Invite your team, assign roles and control generation quotas, so production stays organised and costs stay predictable.
  • The Studio. The creative core — generate, polish, remix, adapt aspect ratios, produce video, add text-to-speech voiceovers and upscale. One product photo in; a full set of commercial assets out.
  • Jarvis, the agentic creative director. A conversational AI that drives the Studio's tools for you. You brief it in plain language and it runs the generation, variation and adaptation steps — the production line you talk to.
  • Projects & campaigns. Organise work by client and campaign so nothing gets lost between briefs.
  • Pro export packs. Turn one source asset into many formats automatically and download them as a single ZIP — ready for every channel.
  • Persistent history. Every asset you generate is kept, searchable and re-usable, so past work is always one click away.
  • Tokenized client portal. A branded space where the agency's own client reviews, approves and comments on each asset — with no account needed. Just a secure link.

The distinctive hook: a built-in ROI report

Keou doesn't only produce the work — it quantifies the saving. A built-in ROI report measures the percentage of cost and time saved on each campaign versus the traditional process. For the agency, that report does double duty: it proves the value internally, and it becomes a ready-made upsell deck to hand to clients. The pitch writes itself, with the agency's own numbers on it.
