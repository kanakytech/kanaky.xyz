United Kingdom

Cold email in the UK. The corporate subscriber rule.

The United Kingdom occupies a middle position: more permissive than much of the European Union for business-to-business email, considerably stricter than the United States. The distinction that decides most cases is who counts as a "corporate subscriber" — and it is narrower than most people assume.

This is not legal advice. It is a plain-language summary of publicly available legislation, written to help you ask better questions. Rules change, enforcement practice varies, and your circumstances may differ. If cold outreach is material to your business, have a lawyer in the relevant jurisdiction review what you are doing.

Two regimes, as in the EU

UK GDPR governs the personal data. The Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations — PECR — govern electronic marketing. Both apply to cold email, and the Information Commissioner's Office enforces them.

The corporate subscriber distinction

PECR's consent requirement for marketing email applies to individual subscribers. Corporate subscribers are treated differently, which is what makes B2B cold email workable in the UK.

Recipient typeTreatment under PECR
Limited companies, LLPs, public bodiesCorporate subscriber — consent not required for marketing email
Sole tradersTreated as individual subscribers
Non-LLP partnershipsTreated as individual subscribers
Personal addresses of any kindIndividual subscriber

The consequence is direct and easy to get wrong: emailing a limited company sits on much firmer ground than emailing a sole trader, and a great many small businesses — including most tradespeople, consultants and independent operators — are sole traders. You need to know the legal form of the business you are contacting, not just that it is a business.

UK GDPR still applies

Even where PECR permits the send, UK GDPR governs the underlying personal data. That means:

  • A lawful basis — normally legitimate interest, with a documented balancing assessment.
  • Transparency — telling the recipient where you got their details and what rights they have, at first contact.
  • The right to object — absolute for direct marketing. On objection you stop permanently.
  • Accurate records of source and basis, which the ICO can ask you to produce.

What every message must do

1
Identify the sender accurately. Concealing or disguising identity is prohibited.
2
Provide a valid address for opt-out requests and honour them without delay.
3
Tell them where you got their details — this is the UK GDPR transparency obligation, and it is the step most often missed.
4
Check the legal form of the business before treating a recipient as a corporate subscriber.
5
Keep the message relevant to the role. Relevance supports the legitimate-interest assessment you are relying on.
The ICO publishes direct marketing guidance in plain English, and it is genuinely readable — unusually so for regulatory material. If you send into the UK regularly, an hour with it is a better investment than any summary, including this one.

Common questions

Can I cold email UK businesses without consent?

For corporate subscribers — limited companies, LLPs and public bodies — PECR does not require consent for marketing email. For sole traders and non-LLP partnerships, who are treated as individual subscribers, the position is much closer to requiring consent. Since many small businesses are sole traders, the legal form of the recipient matters before you send.

How do I know if a business is a corporate subscriber?

Check the company register. UK companies and LLPs are searchable on Companies House, and most businesses display their company number and registered form on their website footer as they are required to. If you cannot establish the legal form, the cautious approach is to treat the recipient as an individual subscriber.

Do I have to say where I got their email address?

Yes. UK GDPR requires you to inform people when you obtain their personal data from a source other than themselves, including the source. In practice this means a brief privacy notice or a clear link in your first message — and it is the most commonly omitted requirement in UK outreach.

What are the penalties?

The ICO can issue monetary penalties under PECR, and separately under UK GDPR where data protection obligations are breached. It publishes enforcement actions, and unsolicited marketing is a recurring category. Complaints from recipients are the usual starting point for an investigation.
