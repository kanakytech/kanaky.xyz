Buying vs renting

Cold email software you buy once, instead of renting every month.

The short answer: yes, this exists, and there is more than one option — including several that aren't ours. Below is what the market actually looks like in July 2026, what a one-time purchase does and doesn't remove from your costs, and the cases where a monthly subscription is genuinely the better decision.

We sell one of these tools. That's a reason to read the comparison carefully, not a reason to expect it to be one-sided — an outbound tool that doesn't fit your situation will waste far more than it costs.

What it costs to own

The one-time-purchase options, including the ones we don't sell.

There are two genuinely different categories here, and confusing them is the most common mistake buyers make.

Category one

Self-hosted senders

Delivery infrastructure you install on your own server. They send reliably, rotate mailboxes, handle warm-up and validation. You supply the prospect list and write every message yourself.

  • Cleanmails — around $199 once
  • Broadcast — around $250 once
  • AcelleMail — from around $80 once

Also worth knowing: Mautic and Postal are open source and free of licence cost — but not free of cost. You pay in hosting and in the engineering hours that deliverability management demands.

Category two

Systems that also find and write

Bought once like the above, but they do the two jobs a sender doesn't: finding the companies, and writing each message individually. That's the work that actually takes your week.

  • Cold Outreach Machine — $499 once

Higher than a pure sender, and the reason is the scope: describing your ideal customer replaces both list-building and copywriting.

What "pay once" never means.

No cold email setup is free to run, whichever licence you buy. Three costs stay with you, and any vendor implying otherwise is selling you a story:

A sending domain

About $12 a year. Use a separate one, never your main company domain — so that if deliverability ever suffers, your invoices and client email are untouched.

A way to send

An SMTP or email service, plus SPF, DKIM and DMARC records verified on that domain. Skip those records and most of what you send lands in spam, no matter how good the software is.

The writing, if AI does it

Either a few cents per email through a cloud AI provider, or nothing per email if you run a local model on your own hardware — which also means your prospect data never leaves your machine.

What a one-time purchase removes is the licence fee — the line that repeats every month whether you sent ten emails or a thousand, and that ends your access the day you stop paying. It does not remove infrastructure.

The honest part

When you should subscribe and not buy.

A subscription platform is the better decision if you need managed inbox warm-up across dozens of mailboxes, a team working in shared dashboards with permissions and reporting, a built-in contact database you'd otherwise have to source separately, or a support desk that answers in minutes rather than hours.

Those are real advantages, and a growing outbound team usually needs them. Buying once fits a different profile: a founder, a consultant, or a small business sending a steady moderate volume, who wants the cost to stop climbing, wants the prospect data to stay on their own machine, and is comfortable with a setup that takes an hour rather than five minutes.

If you're between the two, the deciding question is usually not price. It's whether outbound is something you'll still be doing in a year. If yes, owning it wins on both cost and control. If you're testing whether outbound works at all, rent for a quarter first.

Common questions

Is one-time cold email software cheaper in the long run?

Against a $39/month plan, a $499 purchase pays for itself in about eight months. Against a $97/month plan, in about three. After that the licence line is zero — but remember the infrastructure costs above continue in both models, so the saving is on the fee, not the total.

Do I need to be technical?

For the Cold Outreach Machine: no code, but you do need to be comfortable running a command in a terminal and pasting DNS records into your domain. A guided installer asks for what it needs, explains which accounts to create, and tests each connection before continuing. A live setup call is included, and most buyers finish it with the machine already running.

Is cold email legal?

Business-to-business cold email is lawful in most jurisdictions, under conditions that differ by country — the Unsolicited Electronic Messages Act 2007 in New Zealand, the Spam Act 2003 in Australia, CAN-SPAM in the United States, GDPR and ePrivacy rules in the EU and UK. Two obligations are near-universal: identify yourself genuinely, and stop immediately when asked. Software can enforce the mechanics; the legal responsibility stays with the sender.

Can I resell software I bought outright?

Only if its licence says so, and most don't by default. Some vendors sell resale or SaaS rights as a higher tier. The Cold Outreach Machine licence grants a perpetual right to run and modify the software inside one legal entity, and prohibits reselling, sublicensing, publishing the source, or running it as a service for others.
