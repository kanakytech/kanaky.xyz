Marine

AI for marine businesses. Built around the season.

Marine businesses run on a seasonal cycle that concentrates demand into a few months and leaves the rest quiet. That shape — not the technology — should determine what is worth automating, because the constraint in peak season and the constraint in the off-season are opposite problems.

Peak season: the constraint is capacity

In season, the yard is full and the phone does not stop. The problem is not finding work, it is handling enquiries without losing the ones worth having.

  • Enquiry triage. Distinguishing a hardstand booking from a full refit enquiry from a warranty question, and routing each appropriately.
  • Booking coordination for haul-outs, hardstand and berth allocation, where the constraint is physical space and the scheduling is genuinely complex.
  • Quote turnaround for antifoul, survey work and repairs — dictated at the boat, formatted and sent before you have walked back to the office.
  • Waitlist management, so a cancellation gets filled instead of becoming a gap.

Off-season: the constraint is work

The same business four months later has capacity and no queue. This is when the systems built in season earn their keep.

  • Maintenance reminders driven by service history. Antifoul intervals, engine servicing, rigging inspection, survey renewals. Boat owners forget; the yard that reminds them books the work.
  • Reactivating past customers. Everyone who hauled out two seasons ago and has not returned.
  • Approaching commercial operators. Charter fleets, fishing operations, tourism vessels and marine transport have year-round maintenance obligations and are far less seasonal than private owners. This is the most valuable client category available to a marine business and the least systematically pursued.
  • Quoting refit work — the large winter jobs that make the off-season profitable.

Survey and compliance documentation

Marine work generates substantial documentation: survey reports, compliance certification, insurance documentation, maintenance records that follow a vessel and affect its value. Assembling reports from inspection notes and photographs, and keeping per-vessel service histories current, is mechanical work that currently happens in evenings.

The single most valuable thing for most marine businesses is a per-vessel service history that automatically generates the next reminder. It converts a one-off haul-out into a recurring customer, and it is the difference between a yard that waits for the phone and one that fills its own calendar.

Common questions

What is the highest-value automation for a boatyard?

Maintenance reminders driven by service history. Antifoul intervals, engine servicing and survey renewals are predictable, owners forget them, and the yard that sends the reminder gets the booking. It converts occasional customers into recurring ones and directly fills the off-season.

How do marine businesses find commercial clients?

Charter operators, fishing fleets, tourism vessels and marine transport companies all have year-round maintenance requirements and statutory obligations. They are reachable directly and are far less seasonal than private owners. Very few marine businesses approach them systematically, which is exactly why it works.

Can AI help with survey reports?

It can assemble a structured report from inspection notes and photographs, which removes hours of writing up. The findings and the judgement remain the surveyor's — and where survey work carries certification obligations, the qualified person signs and is responsible for what is in it.

Is this worth it for a small operation?

The reminder system alone usually is, because it directly generates bookings rather than merely saving time. Start there — it pays for itself in filled slots — and add quoting once you see the pattern.
