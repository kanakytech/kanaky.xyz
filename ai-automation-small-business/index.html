Getting started

AI automation for a small business. What to do first.

Most small businesses adopting AI start with whatever they read about most recently, which is usually a chatbot, which is usually the wrong place to start. A better method: find where hours actually disappear, then automate the least interesting one.

On this page
  1. Find the hours before choosing a tool
  2. What usually surfaces
  3. What to avoid at the start
  4. Judging whether it worked

Find the hours before choosing a tool

  1. Track a week. Note what you and your team actually do, in half-hour blocks. Everyone is surprised by this, and the surprise is the point.
  2. Mark anything repetitive. Same shape, different details. Copying between systems, re-typing, chasing, formatting, scheduling.
  3. Add up the hours per task per month. Small tasks done daily beat large ones done occasionally — this is where intuition is most often wrong.
  4. Rank by hours, not by how annoying it is. The most irritating task is rarely the most expensive one.
  5. Start at the top of that list. Not with the most interesting technology.

What usually surfaces

The same categories appear in most small businesses, regardless of industry:

  • Quoting and proposals — high value, done at night, slow to go out.
  • Enquiry response — where speed decides who wins the job.
  • Data re-entry between systems — the purest waste in any business.
  • Chasing — invoices, quotes, missing information. Universally avoided, universally valuable.
  • Reporting — assembled by hand, monthly, by someone who could be doing something else.
  • Scheduling — rosters, appointments, coordination.

What to avoid at the start

  • Customer-facing chatbots. High visibility, high risk to your reputation, usually low actual saving. If it goes wrong, your customers see it.
  • Replacing something that works. Automating a functioning process to modernise it is how you buy a problem you did not have.
  • Broad rollouts. One workflow, working properly, beats five half-configured.
  • Tools looking for problems. If you cannot name the hours it saves before buying, do not buy it.

Judging whether it worked

Measure the same thing before and after: hours spent, response time, error rate, or revenue where the link is direct — a quote that goes out same-day rather than next-week has a measurable conversion effect.

If you cannot measure a difference after a month, either it is not saving what you thought or it is not being used. Both are worth knowing, and both are common.

Realistic expectation for a first project: one process, five to fifteen hours a month back, working within a few weeks. Anyone promising a transformed business is selling something. A few hours a month, compounding across several processes, is what actually changes a small business over a year.

Common questions

What should a small business automate first?

Whichever repetitive task consumes the most hours per month — which you will only know after tracking a week honestly. For most small businesses it turns out to be quoting, enquiry response, or copying data between systems. Start there rather than with whatever technology is most discussed.

How much does it cost to get started?

Less than most expect for a single workflow, and it varies with what you already use. The larger cost is usually configuring it around how your business actually works rather than the software itself. Start with one process so the commitment matches the uncertainty.

Do I need technical staff?

For off-the-shelf tools, no. For anything connecting your existing systems together, you need either technical capability or someone to build it. The honest signal that you need help is when the answer involves the phrase "and then it needs to talk to".

What if it does not work?

Then you have learned something specific and cheap, provided you started with one process rather than five. The expensive failure is a broad rollout that nobody adopts — which is why a narrow first project with a measurable before-and-after is the right shape regardless of the technology.
