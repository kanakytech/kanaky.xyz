Trades

AI for plumbers. Built around callouts, not projects.

Plumbing runs on a different rhythm from most trades. Work arrives unplanned and often urgent, the phone rings while you are under a sink, and the difference between a good week and a bad one is frequently how well the interruptions were handled rather than how well the work was done.

Triage is the highest-value thing here

Not every call is an emergency, and not every emergency is yours. A burst pipe at 2am and a slow-draining shower need completely different responses, but both arrive on the same phone.

Automated triage — capturing the enquiry, asking the two or three questions that establish urgency, and either scheduling it or flagging it for immediate attention — is worth more in plumbing than in trades where work is planned weeks ahead. What it protects is not time so much as attention: you stop breaking off a job to discover the call was a quote request.

Quoting from the van

Same pattern as other trades, with a plumbing-specific wrinkle: much of the work is diagnosed on site, and the quote depends on what was found. Dictating "replace hot water cylinder, supply 180 litre, two isolation valves, half day two people, plus callout" and getting back a formatted quote before you have left the driveway means the customer decides while you are still standing there.

The invoice problem

Plumbing has a debtor problem that project trades do not, because the work is done before anyone discusses money and emergency customers are less price-sensitive at 2am than they are on Friday. Automated, polite, escalating payment reminders recover money that would otherwise be written off — and they do it without the awkward phone call most people avoid making.

Maintenance contracts are the underused asset

  • Reminders for annual servicing — cylinders, backflow prevention, commercial grease traps.
  • Compliance schedules for commercial clients, who are legally obliged and frequently disorganised.
  • Reactivating past customers. A message to everyone you served two years ago costs nothing and reliably produces work.
  • Approaching property managers. Rental portfolios generate constant plumbing work and value reliability over price — the best commercial relationship available to a plumber, and rarely pursued systematically.
Nothing here diagnoses a leak or decides what compliant work looks like. The value sits entirely in the layer around the job: the enquiry, the quote, the invoice and the follow-up.

Common questions

Can AI answer my phone?

It can capture enquiries, ask qualifying questions and schedule non-urgent work, then route genuine emergencies to you immediately. Whether you want it on your main line is a judgement call — some customers accept it, others want a human, and in emergency plumbing the second group is larger. Many businesses use it for overflow and after-hours rather than as the front line.

Does it help with unpaid invoices?

Yes, and this is often the fastest payback in plumbing specifically. Automated escalating reminders recover money that otherwise gets written off, and they remove the reluctance that stops most sole operators from chasing at all.

What about emergency callouts at night?

Automated triage that captures details, establishes urgency and wakes you only for genuine emergencies is a real quality-of-life improvement. The rule that matters: err toward waking you. A missed real emergency costs far more than an unnecessary alert.

Is it worth it if I only do residential work?

The quoting and invoice-chasing pieces are, immediately. The commercial and maintenance-contract pieces are where residential plumbers often find a more profitable second stream — property managers in particular, who need someone reliable on call and are reachable directly.
