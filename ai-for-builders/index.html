Trades

AI for builders. The problem is the paperwork trail.

Building differs from callout trades in one decisive way: projects run for months, involve many parties, and generate a documentation trail that becomes the evidence when something is disputed. The admin burden is not a series of small tasks — it is one long one, and it is where margin quietly disappears.

Variations are where money is lost

Most builders can point to a job where the variations were agreed verbally, done, and then argued about at invoicing. The work was real; the record was not.

Capturing variations as they happen — a voice note on site, converted into a dated written record with scope and cost, sent to the client for confirmation — is unglamorous and directly protects margin. This is the single highest-value automation available to a building company, and it is worth more than anything on the estimating side.

Estimating support

Not automated estimating — quantities and pricing need someone who knows the job. But extracting a materials schedule from plans and specifications, and checking a new estimate against what similar past jobs actually cost, removes hours of reading and gives you a sanity check that catches the estimate that drifted.

Coordination and client updates

  • Subcontractor scheduling. Confirmations, reminders, and rescheduling messages when a trade runs late — which cascades into every trade behind them.
  • Weekly client updates. Generated from site notes and photos. Clients who receive a regular update ring far less often, and the calls they do make are better ones.
  • Compliance documentation. Building consent conditions, inspection records, producer statements — assembled from what you already recorded rather than re-typed.
  • Health and safety records. Site inductions, toolbox talks, hazard registers. Legally required, universally resented, entirely mechanical.
  • Defect lists captured on site and turned into scheduled work with responsibilities attached.

Finding the next project

Building companies typically rely on referrals and repeat clients, which works until it does not — usually at exactly the wrong point in the cycle. Architects, developers, property managers and commercial clients are all reachable directly, and a builder with a genuine specialisation who approaches them systematically is unusual enough to be noticed.

None of this touches the build. It touches the record of the build, which is where disputes are won or lost and where a surprising share of a builder's week actually goes.

Common questions

Can AI do my estimating?

It can extract materials schedules from plans and compare a draft estimate against what your past jobs actually cost, which catches the ones that have drifted. It cannot judge site conditions, buildability or risk — those are why estimators exist. Treat it as a fast first pass and a cross-check, not an estimator.

What is the fastest payback for a building company?

Variation capture. Recording verbally agreed changes as dated written records, confirmed by the client, protects margin directly and prevents the disputes that cost far more than any admin time. Most builders lose more money to undocumented variations than to any inefficiency automation would fix.

Will it help with health and safety compliance?

It can generate and maintain the documentation — inductions, toolbox talk records, hazard registers — from what happens on site. It does not make your site safe, and it does not replace your obligations. It removes the excuse that the paperwork is too tedious to keep current, which is when it actually becomes evidence.

How do builders find commercial work?

Architects, developers, property managers and facilities companies commission continuously and are reachable by email. The builders who succeed at this have a specific angle — a build type, a compliance capability, a regional advantage — rather than a general offer. See building an ideal customer profile.
