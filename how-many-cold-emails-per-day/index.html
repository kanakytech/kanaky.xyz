Volume and pacing

How many cold emails a day is actually safe?

For an established domain with authentication in place, twenty to fifty a day per mailbox is a defensible range. For a new domain, start with five. Those numbers are lower than most tools imply, and the reason is worth understanding — because the cost of getting this wrong is not a bad campaign, it is a burnt domain.

Why the safe number is low

Receiving servers do not judge a single message. They judge a pattern: how much you send, how fast, how consistently, how many bounce, how many people complain, and how many reply. A domain sending five messages a day for a month and a domain sending a hundred and fifty on a Tuesday send the same volume — but only one of them looks like a business.

The asymmetry matters. Building a good sending reputation takes weeks. Losing it takes an afternoon. And a flagged domain does not just hurt your outreach — if it is anywhere near your main domain, it hurts your invoices too.

Pace matters as much as volume

Twenty emails sent in four minutes and twenty emails spread across a working day are the same volume and completely different signals. Space them fifteen to thirty minutes apart, keep them inside business hours in the recipient's timezone, and send on weekdays only. A human writing twenty individual emails could not physically do it any faster — that is the shape you are imitating, because that is the shape that is not suspicious.

If you need more volume

Add mailboxes or domains rather than pushing one harder. Ten mailboxes sending twenty a day is a far safer two hundred than one mailbox sending two hundred. This is exactly what large outbound teams do, and it is the point at which a managed platform with multi-inbox warm-up starts earning its subscription.

Before adding volume, ask whether volume is the constraint. If a hundred well-researched emails produce nothing, a thousand produce nothing louder. Poor targeting scales badly, and the fix is upstream — in who you are writing to, not how many.

Common questions

What happens if I send too many too fast?

The realistic outcomes, roughly in order: messages start landing in spam, then bounces climb as providers throttle you, then your domain accumulates a negative reputation that follows it. Recovery means stopping entirely, fixing the underlying cause, and warming up again slowly over weeks — or buying a new domain and starting over.

Do these limits apply per mailbox or per domain?

Both, and they interact. Individual mailboxes have provider limits, while reputation attaches largely to the domain. Running five mailboxes on one domain at fifty a day each means that domain is sending two hundred and fifty a day, and it will be judged on that total.

Is there a hard limit imposed by email providers?

Providers do publish sending caps, and those are real, but they are ceilings for all mail rather than guidance for cold outreach specifically. Staying under a provider limit tells you nothing about whether your reputation is healthy. The safe cold-email number is well below any published cap.

Should I send at the same time every day?

Roughly, but not exactly. A perfectly regular pattern — the same messages at the same second every day — is itself a machine signature. Some natural variation within business hours is both more human and slightly safer.
